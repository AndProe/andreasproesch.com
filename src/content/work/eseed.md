When I left Management Consulting, I started helping a couple of startups very informally. A few friends had started companies and I'd help where I could: a sounding board on a pitch deck, an intro from my own network, brainstorming when something at the company was going wrong. I didn't have money in anything; I was mostly doing it to help out, and then it turned into being really fun.

After a while, I got in contact with a few INSEAD alumni who had built an angel syndicate called eSeed. Every deal runs as its own SPV, so you're not committing blind to a fund — you pick the companies you want to get involved in, and the syndicate pulls together whoever in the network wants in on that specific round. After a few deals, I also joined the management team, and we started going global.

What angel syndicates do differently from a standalone angel cheque is scale. A cheque from one person usually doesn't move much at pre-seed. But pulling together twenty or thirty people's networks behind a founder — customer intros, help with the raise, support with hiring? That's a different story.

We look for founders where that kind of help changes the trajectory, not just ones who need the cash. Since inception we've backed companies across Europe, South America, and the Middle East.

Running those SPVs was my early education in startup investing: deal flow generation, due diligence, term sheet negotiations, supporting founders during rounds. That's the part I wasn't doing when I was just a friend with an opinion. I now write cheques as a solo angel too, working alongside a few other angel networks depending on the sector and stage. But eSeed is where it started for me.
